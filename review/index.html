PNFSurf: Gaussian Surface Reconstruction Guided by Projective Normal Fields

Comparisons Videos Method

Abstract

Recovering coherent surfaces from 3D Gaussian representations remains challenging when weak texture, occlusion, and uneven camera coverage leave local geometry ambiguous. We introduce PNFSurf, a projective normal field framework for multi-view surface reconstruction. A visibility- and appearance-guided graph aggregates normal-axis evidence across compatible Gaussians, while Gaussian-aware anchors combine covariance-derived axes with neighborhood geometry. A rendering term aligns alpha-composited field tensors with normal projectors derived from the current rendered depth, incorporating image-space geometric consistency into field inference. With geometry and rendering evidence fixed and positive anchoring weights, the field objective is strongly convex and admits a unique global minimizer. We alternate field inference with Gaussian optimization, using the inferred field to guide orientations and positions while enforcing depth-normal and cross-view consistency. Experiments span object benchmarks (DTU and NeRF-Synthetic), indoor benchmarks (Replica, ScanNet++, and MuSHRoom), and mixed indoor/outdoor benchmarks (Tanks and Temples and Mip-NeRF 360). Integrations with PGSR and AmbiSuR improve surface reconstruction quality over their respective backbones, while rendering evaluations show competitive performance. These results demonstrate the benefits of normal-field guidance across different Gaussian reconstruction pipelines.
