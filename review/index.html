Abstract

Indoor scenes contain large, weakly textured surfaces whose geometry is difficult to recover from photometric supervision alone. Occlusions further complicate the aggregation of local geometric cues. We introduce VisPNF, a visibility- and appearance-guided extension of point-cloud Projective Normal Fields (PNF) for indoor Gaussian surface reconstruction. A weighted graph combines geometric compatibility, shared view coverage, opacity, and radiance similarity to coordinate sign-ambiguous normal axes across compatible Gaussians. Gaussian-aware anchors provide local orientation cues, while a rendering term aligns alpha-composited field tensors with depth-derived normal projectors. With geometric and rendering evidence fixed and positive anchoring weights, the PNF objective is strongly convex and admits a unique minimizer. We alternate field inference with Gaussian optimization, using the inferred field to guide orientations and positions and rendered-projector losses to enforce depth-normal and cross-view consistency. Experiments on Replica, ScanNet++, and MuSHRoom demonstrate consistent improvements in reconstruction and rendering over the PGSR backbone.

Method overview

Visibility- and appearance-guided geometric evidence. A weighted graph combines spatial proximity, normal-axis agreement, shared visibility, depth compatibility, Gaussian opacity, and radiance similarity. Its weights favor compatible evidence while reducing coupling across surface boundaries.

Iterative field–geometry coupling. Sign-invariant projectors retain normal-axis ambiguity. The inferred field provides detached targets for Gaussian orientations and positions, while rendered projectors enforce depth-normal and cross-view consistency. Periodic updates follow the evolving geometry, with each fixed-data field subproblem remaining convex.
