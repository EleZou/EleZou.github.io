Abstract

Recovering coherent surfaces from 3D Gaussians is challenging because each Gaussian provides only a local and sign-ambiguous estimate of surface orientation, while spatially nearby cues may become unreliable across visibility and depth discontinuities. To address this, we introduce Projective Gaussian Surface Fields (PGSF), a projective formulation that organizes local Gaussian geometry into a shared field for surface reconstruction. Specifically, we represent each Gaussian orientation with a sign-invariant rank-one projector and, based on these projective cues, infer a soft Projective Normal Field (PNF) over visibility- and depth-aware neighborhoods. In this way, the field aggregates compatible geometric evidence while preserving uncertainty where local observations are ambiguous. We then use the inferred field as a detached geometric target to regularize Gaussian orientations and positions; meanwhile, rendering the projector field provides depth-normal and cross-view consistency. Together, these components provide a structured mechanism for propagating local geometric evidence while decoupling field inference from non-convex Gaussian optimization. Experiments on Replica, ScanNet++, and MuSHRoom demonstrate consistent improvements over PGSR in surface reconstruction and rendering quality across diverse indoor environments.

Method overview

A field that respects orientation ambiguity. Sign-invariant projectors represent Gaussian axes without assigning an arbitrary normal direction. Visibility- and depth-aware neighborhoods bring compatible cues together while preserving uncertainty.

Geometry coupled across space and views. The inferred field regularizes Gaussian orientations and positions. Depth-normal and cross-view consistency connect the rendered projectors to a shared surface.
